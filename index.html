Step 1 · 3 min read

What is happening in India

Since mid-2025 India’s Election Commission has run a Special Intensive Revision (SIR) of the voter rolls, first in Bihar and then nationwide. Critics report that around 130 million names have been removed. The Election Commission says the exercise corrects inaccurate rolls. The ruling BJP denies any manipulation.

The youth-led Cockroach Janta Party (CJP) began in May 2026, after a remark by the Chief Justice comparing unemployed young people to cockroaches. With students and opposition parties, it now demands the resignation of Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar.

The state response in October 2026 has been severe. Authorities closed 57 metro stations in Delhi, cancelled trains and suspended mobile internet around the protest area. Thousands of people were detained over the weekend of 10 and 11 October. The movement’s three leaders were stopped on arrival at Delhi airport and later released on condition of “no breach of peace for one year”.

Your strongest hook

On 11 October 2026 the Supreme Court of India issued an interim order that people assembling peacefully must not be illegally detained. It comes from India’s own highest court, so build every ask around it.

The advocacy window

  1. Mid-2025SIR of voter rolls begins in Bihar, then goes nationwide.
  2. 27 Jan 2026EU and India conclude their free trade agreement.
  3. May 2026Cockroach Janta Party movement begins.
  4. 11 Sep 2026European Commission sends the FTA to the Council for signature.
  5. 10–11 Oct 2026Thousands detained in New Delhi. Supreme Court interim order on 11 October.
  6. NextCouncil signature, then European Parliament consent. This is when EU institutions pay the most attention to India.

Be realistic

Germany strongly supports the agreement, and it is very unlikely to be blocked over this issue. Aim for attention: questions on the record, statements alongside the signature, and human rights language in Parliament’s debate and resolutions.

Step 2 · Read before you write

Five ground rules

Tap each one for the reasoning.

Do Anchor every ask in India’s own law
Cite Article 19(1)(b) of the Indian Constitution (the right to assemble peaceably), Article 326 (adult suffrage), the Supreme Court’s 11 October order, and India’s obligations under the International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights, which it ratified in 1979.
Don’t Ask European actors to call for a resignation
That is a decision for Indian citizens and Indian institutions. A foreign government making the demand hands the Indian government a ready-made “foreign interference” story.
Do Talk to the movement first
In 2021 Indian police treated a protest “toolkit” shared by activists abroad as evidence of conspiracy, and a climate activist was arrested over it. Before scaling up any action, ask organisers in India whether international attention helps them right now, and in what form.
Do Be specific
Policy staff act on numbers, dates and named court orders. Vague concern about “civil society” gets a polite form reply.
Do Personalise every letter
Identical emails are logged as a campaign and given less weight than one considered letter. The builder in Step 4 gives you a starting point. Add a sentence in your own words.

Indian nationals: weigh the personal risk first.

Indian authorities have cancelled the Overseas Citizen of India (OCI) status of some critics living abroad, and passport and consular services can become harder to use. Families in India can also face pressure.

  • Decide individually whether to sign publicly. An organisation or coalition can front a letter instead.
  • Check with your employer if your role links you publicly to an organisation.
  • Coordinate on Signal. Do not keep lists of participants in openly shared documents.

Step 3 · Pick one target to start

Who to contact, in order of leverage

European Parliament

Where the main decision sits.

  • INTA (Committee on International Trade), especially the rapporteur for the EU-India agreement. INTA prepares the consent recommendation.
  • DROI (Subcommittee on Human Rights). Ask them to press for an urgency resolution if detentions continue. Precedent: Manipur, July 2023.
  • Delegation for relations with India.
  • MEPs who live or keep offices in Berlin. Germany elects MEPs from party lists, so there is no single “Berlin MEP”.

Ask for one thing: raise the Supreme Court order in the consent debate, table a written question, or support an urgency resolution.

Bundestag

Your own MdB is the easiest door to open.

  • Your local MdB. Every MdB can submit up to four schriftliche Fragen a month. Send them ready-drafted questions. A finished question is easy to say yes to.
  • An individual MdB cannot file a Kleine Anfrage. That needs a parliamentary group or 5% of members. Ask them to take it to their group.
  • Members of the Ausschuss für Menschenrechte und humanitäre Hilfe, especially South Asia rapporteurs.

Ask for: one or more of the written questions in Step 5.

Find your MdB by postcode ↗

Federal Government

Auswärtiges Amt and Germany’s Council vote.

  • Commissioner for Human Rights Policy and Humanitarian Assistance at the Federal Foreign Office. Ask that freedom of assembly, internet shutdowns and detentions be raised with Indian counterparts and in the EU–India human rights dialogue.
  • Germany’s Council vote. Germany will back signature. Ask that it come with a clear public statement on freedom of assembly and the right to vote.

Step 5 · Attach these

        
Ready-to-submit questions and the briefing

        
Send the questions with your letter. Staff can forward them as written.

      

      

        
        
      


      

        
Schriftliche Fragen. Each is short and answerable. Your MdB can submit up to four a month.

        
Frage 1
Hat die Bundesregierung die einstweilige Anordnung des Obersten Gerichtshofs Indiens vom 11. Oktober 2026 zum Schutz friedlicher Versammlungen gegenüber der indischen Regierung angesprochen, und wenn ja, in welchem Rahmen?
Has the government raised the 11 October Supreme Court order with India, and in what forum?

        
Frage 2
Welche Erkenntnisse hat die Bundesregierung über die Zahl der seit dem 1. Oktober 2026 in Indien im Zusammenhang mit Protesten gegen die Überarbeitung der Wählerverzeichnisse festgenommenen Personen sowie über die Sperrung mobiler Internetdienste in Neu-Delhi?
What does the government know about detentions since 1 October and the mobile internet shutdown in New Delhi?

        
Frage 3
Wird sich die Bundesregierung im Rat dafür einsetzen, dass die Unterzeichnung des Freihandelsabkommens zwischen der EU und Indien von einer öffentlichen Erklärung zur Versammlungsfreiheit und zum Wahlrecht begleitet wird?
Will Germany push in the Council for a public statement on assembly and voting rights alongside FTA signature?

        
Frage 4
Wird die Bundesregierung darauf hinwirken, dass die Versammlungs- und Meinungsfreiheit in Indien im nächsten Menschenrechtsdialog der EU mit Indien thematisiert wird?
Will the government push for these freedoms to be on the agenda of the next EU–India human rights dialogue?

        
 

      


      

        
Parliamentary written questions are limited to 200 words and three sub-questions. This one fits.

        

          

            
Subject: Freedom of assembly in India and the EU-India Free Trade Agreement

            
On 11 October 2026 the Supreme Court of India directed that people assembling peacefully must not be illegally detained. In the preceding days thousands of protesters were detained in New Delhi, and mobile internet was suspended around protest sites. The protests concern a revision of the voter rolls that critics say removed around 130 million names.

            
    
              
  1. Has the Commission, or the Vice-President/High Representative, raised these events with the Government of India?
